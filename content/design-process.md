Before adding data to the platform we need to understand the users, needs and benefits Discovery starts with a topic, consideration, user, or their objective.

### Discovery

In discovery you will need to do some research and to document what you learn.
AI can help, but it is essential you research the users, particularly:

* producers of the data
* consumers of the data
* policy makers and other key stakeholders

Discovery is complete when you have documented the following:

* users, and their needs for data
* needs which are relevant to planning and housing
* all relevant legislation, policy, sources of information and data
* the authority responsible for creating, updating and removing each entity being recorded
* <i>something about estimating the number of entities, how often they change</i>
* any security or privacy concerns
* how collecting the [data is lawful](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/public-task/), and within the public task of the department
* existing sources of the data along with barriers to collecting existing data, such as restrictive licensing

Against each need:

* <i>something about how this supports the missions better decision making and faster local plans</i>
* <i>something about the benefits</i>

At the end of discovery you will have documented your research made it open for feedback from users.

### Alpha

Alpha is complete when you have demonstrated the following:

* proposed entity, fieldnames and other terms
* the key entities
* draft a specification and dataset schemas
* collect data from existing sources
* Build a prototype using the data you have collected to:
  - prove the specification
  - shown the value of the data to users and stakeholders
* Test the prototype with users
* Document how the specification meets the user needs

### Beta

* work with providers of the data

### Live

* continue to evolve the specifications
