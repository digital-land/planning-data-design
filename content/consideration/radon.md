## Screen

### What is the 'Radon' consideration?

"Radon is a radioactive gas, we can't see, smell or taste it: you need special equipment to detect it. It comes from the rocks and soil found everywhere in the UK. The radon level in the air we breathe outside is very low but can be higher inside buildings." - [UKRadon](https://www.ukradon.org/information/)

### Is there legislation that defines 'Radon'?

Yes

### What is the legislation that defines 'Radon'?

NOT LEGISLATION - [Building Regulations 2004](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/431943/BR_PDF_AD_C_2013.pdf), paragraps 2.39 and 2.40.

### What is the specific part of the legislation that describes how a 'Radon' gets designated?

[Building Regulations 2004](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/431943/BR_PDF_AD_C_2013.pdf) - "BR 211 provides guidance on basic radon protective measures appropriate in areas where 3% - 10% of homes and full radon protective measures in areas where more than 10% of homes are predicted to have radon at or above the Radon Action Level of 200Bq/m3."

### What are the planning needs for this data?

Understanding the presence of radon is essential for homeowners, landowners and developers, to ensure that sufficient mitigation is taken to prevent any harmful effects.

### Who are the types of users who might benefit from having access to Radon data?

Developers - so that they can prepare the correct mitigation

Planning officers so that they know mitigations are required

Citizens/conveyancers need to know Radon levels for searches

### What are some potential uses of Radon data?

Understanding mitigating Radon levels is important for building controls - as referenced in this [guidance document](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/431943/BR_PDF_AD_C_2013.pdf)
