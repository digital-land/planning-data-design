## Screen

### What is the 'Main rivers' consideration?

Main Rivers are a legal designation relating to watercourses.

Main rivers are usually larger rivers and streams. They are designated as such, and shown on the Main River Map. The Environment Agency carries out maintenance, improvement or construction work on main rivers to manage flood risk. Other rivers are called ‘ordinary watercourses’. Lead local flood authorities, district councils and internal drainage boards carry out flood risk management work on ordinary watercourses.

### Is there legislation that defines 'Main rivers'?

Yes

### What is the legislation that defines 'Main rivers'?

Water Resources Act 1991

### What is the specific part of the legislation that describes how a 'Main rivers' gets designated?

[Guidance under S193E outlines how Environment Agency designates Main River](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/designation-of-main-rivers-guidance-to-the-environment-agency/designation-of-main-rivers-guidance-to-the-environment-agency)

### What is the specific part of the legislation that requires the publication of 'Main rivers'?

The [Water Resources Act 1991, Section 193A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/57/part/VIII/crossheading/main-river-map-for-england) states that "The Agency must provide reasonable facilities for inspecting the main river map for England in electronic form."

### What are the planning needs for this data?

In both policy and DM planning the presence of rivers indicates other constraints and considerations, such as; flood plain, nutrient neutrality requirements and biodiversity. The river itself is a constraint that would be considered when looking for suitable sites to develop.
